# Landscape notes: how Indian students actually learn today

> STATUS: general knowledge, NOT survey data. Nothing below is a per-student
> statistic. Treat as orientation for the counselor, not as facts about any user.

## The big picture
- **YouTube is the default classroom.** For most Indian school and college students,
  free YouTube lectures (Hindi, Hinglish, and English) are the first resort — before
  textbooks, before paid courses. Channels run by coaching institutes (Physics Wallah,
  Unacademy, Vedantu and their subject-specific arms) dominate exam-prep viewing.
- **Government free infrastructure is large but under-discovered.** NPTEL/SWAYAM
  (IIT/IISc-level courses, free, optional paid certification), DIKSHA and ePathshala
  (NCERT-aligned school content) are genuinely good and free, but many students only
  find them when a teacher points them there. The counselor should proactively surface
  them — they are the highest value-per-rupee (zero rupees) option.
- **Doubt-solving apps** (photo-a-click-a-doubt style) are heavily used in classes
  9-12, especially for Maths/Physics/Chemistry.
- **Coaching is the paid default** in exam-prep years (JEE/NEET/UPSC), increasingly
  via affordable online batches rather than offline centers. Price sensitivity is high;
  "free YouTube + one cheap batch" is the common stack.
- **Telegram/WhatsApp study groups** function as the real "community layer" —
  notes sharing, PYQs, motivation. Quality varies wildly; the counselor should prefer
  groups attached to known educators over random forwards.
- **English-skill and Excel/tally-type upskilling** is the quiet parallel track for
  commerce students and job seekers — high ROI, low glamour, often overlooked by
  counselors that only talk about degrees.

## Urban/rural and income gradients (qualitative)
- Metro students: more paid courses, English-medium resources, 1:1 tutoring.
- Small-town/rural students: overwhelmingly free YouTube + Hindi/Hinglish content;
  data cost matters; downloadable/offline-friendly resources win.
- Never assume a student's budget. The KB marks every entry's cost and language so
  the counselor can filter instead of assuming.

## What this means for PathMind's counselor
1. Default to free, verify quality (that's what the KB is for).
2. Match language first (Hindi/Hinglish vs English) — comprehension beats prestige.
3. Surface NPTEL/SWAYAM/DIKSHA deliberately; students under-use them.
4. For exam prep, pair one structured source (batch/course) with free practice
   (PYQs, NTA Abhyas) rather than stacking five overlapping courses.
5. Re-check paid prices at recommendation time — the KB's prices were live on
   2026-10-15 and will drift.
